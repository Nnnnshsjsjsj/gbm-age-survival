Glioblastoma survival calculator

Predicted overall survival from age at diagnosis and up to four clinical variables, using Cox models fitted on two public research cohorts.

Read first
Educational tool built from retrospective public data. It is not medical advice and must not guide treatment. The models separate groups of patients, not individuals: a concordance index near 0.6–0.7 means two random patients are ranked correctly roughly two times in three.

How the prediction is made, and what the two cohorts show

For each cohort we fitted a family of Cox proportional hazards models: one for every combination of the optional variables (sex, Karnofsky score, MGMT methylation, IDH status), with age always included. When you leave a variable unspecified, the calculator picks the model that uses exactly the variables you did provide, fitted on the patients for whom those variables were recorded. That is why the sample size in "Model used" changes as you add or remove inputs.

The predicted curve is S(t) = S₀(t)exp(β·(x − x̄)), where S₀ is the baseline survival of the cohort's average patient, β the fitted coefficients and x̄ the covariate means. The shaded band is a 95% interval from the covariance of the coefficients on the complementary log-log scale. It covers uncertainty in the coefficients only, not uncertainty in the baseline curve, differences between the cohort's treatment era and today's practice, or variables the models never saw, such as extent of resection or treatment received.

TCGA-GBM: The Cancer Genome Atlas glioblastoma cohort via cBioPortal (study gbm_tcga), diagnoses 1989–2013, mostly United States. CGGA: Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas, mRNAseq_693 and mRNAseq_325 clinical tables, restricted to primary WHO grade IV glioblastoma. CGGA records radiotherapy and temozolomide but not Karnofsky score, so the KPS input is disabled for that cohort. Models were fitted with lifelines (Python); the analysis code accompanies the paper this tool belongs to.
