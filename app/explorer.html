How overall survival differed between age groups in four public research cohorts, side by side.

Read first

This page shows how groups of patients in old research datasets fared. It does not estimate anyone's individual outcome, and it must not be used to make decisions about a person's care.

The patients were diagnosed between 1989 and 2019. Treatment has changed since then, and every group contains people who lived far longer than its median.

Is the age effect the same everywhere?

Hazard ratio per additional year of age from our four cohorts and from published series. A value of 1.03 means each extra year of age raised the risk of death by about 3% within that cohort.

How to read these curves

Each curve is a Kaplan–Meier estimate: the share of a group still alive at each month after diagnosis. It drops when a patient in the group died, and it flattens where patients were still alive at their last visit.

The shaded bands are 95% confidence intervals. Where two bands overlap a lot, the data cannot tell the groups apart.

A group with fewer than 10 patients is not shown, because its curve would say more about individuals than about a group.

Data sources and code
